# Abstract

Optical satellites, the standard tool for mapping floods from space, are structurally blind during the exact conditions when floods are most likely: heavy rain, heavy cloud cover, and Rwanda's rainy seasons bring both at once. Radar satellites like Sentinel-1 see through cloud cover and are freely available, making them a natural alternative — one already validated for Rwanda's flatter river floodplains, but untested in the country's steep western terrain, where a 2023 flood and landslide disaster killed over 130 people.

This brief builds and runs a simple, entirely free Sentinel-1 flood detection method over that steep terrain for the May 2–3, 2023 Western Province floods, reporting an estimated 69 hectares of flooding (with a 45–150 hectare range depending on threshold choice). It also reports an honest failure: an automatic thresholding method that worked in prior, flatter-terrain research broke down here for well-understood statistical reasons. From there, the brief connects these technical findings to a concrete economic question: what would it cost to close the remaining gaps — faster detection, more reliable results, the ability to see flooded buildings — using verified commercial pricing and a review of underused free-access pathways, including the International Charter for Space and Major Disasters and ESA's Third Party Missions program.

The result is not a claim that free satellite data can or can't handle disaster response in Rwanda. It's a specific, numbers-based answer to where free data's real limits sit, and what it would concretely cost an institution like Rwanda's Ministry in Charge of Emergency Management to close them.
